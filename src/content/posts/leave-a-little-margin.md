When I built this theme I gave myself one simple goal: let an article look like an article, and stop it looking like a card stuffed with buttons.

So a lot of things came out. Comments. Analytics beyond a view count. Shortcodes nobody used. Features that looked impressive and had never been clicked. What stayed was not much: a title, running text, a table of contents, tags, a little necessary interaction, and enough empty space.

Reading costs something. If it costs something, a reader should not have to walk through a pile of unrelated things before reaching the first sentence.

## The margin is part of the page

The idea the layout is built on is that the margin belongs to the page rather than sitting empty beside it. On a wide screen, notes hang in the margin the way a reader pencils them there; the outline lives on the right; the site's name stands upright on a spine at the left, the way a title stands on the spine of a book.

None of this is decoration. Each of those columns carries something a reader might want and does not want in the way: the date, the reading time, the chapter you are in, the way back.

On a narrow screen all of it folds into the text instead of disappearing. A phone gets the same information in a different order, which is the whole job.

## What a theme is actually for

A theme is a set of decisions about attention. Every element you add asks the reader to spend some of theirs, and the exchange has to be worth it.

That is why the table of contents is quiet until you look at it, why the theme toggle reveals from the button rather than flipping the whole page, why the share controls sit at the end of the article rather than floating beside it. None of these is a rule about taste. They are answers to the question of what deserves to interrupt a paragraph.

## A note on the margin, literally

This theme is called 纸上留白 — blank space left on paper. The name is the argument. A page is not improved by filling it.

Writing this out makes it sound more deliberate than it was. Mostly I deleted things until the page stopped feeling crowded, and then I stopped.
