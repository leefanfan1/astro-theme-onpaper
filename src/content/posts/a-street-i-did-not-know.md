It was not quite dark when I left the office. The crowd at the subway entrance pushed me to the right. I should have turned left and taken the way home I have walked so many times I could do it with my eyes shut. That evening I followed the crowd a few steps further for no reason I can name, and by the time I noticed, I was standing on a street I did not know.

I checked my phone. Nine minutes to walk back if I turned around now. Nine minutes is not much, so I locked the screen and decided not to go back yet.

The street was narrow, the sort where two cars would have to negotiate. There was a laundry with its door propped open and a woman folding sheets inside, and a shop selling nothing but light bulbs, and a man sitting on a low stool outside his own doorway doing nothing in particular. Nobody looked at me. That was the good part.

I walked to the end of it and found a small square I had never seen, with three trees and a bench and a cat that did not move when I sat down. I stayed for about ten minutes.

I have been thinking about why that evening stuck. Nothing happened. I did not meet anyone or discover anything. The street was not beautiful, only ordinary. But for ten minutes I was somewhere I had no reason to be, and that turned out to be enough to make the day feel different from the ones around it.

Most of what fills a life is not the things we plan. It is the nine extra minutes.

There is a bench in that square. I have been back twice. The cat was there the second time.
